# Floor Fracture

Wais Almakaleh · CSCI 379, Design and Development for XR · Meta Quest 3

[Master project checklist](<./Floor Fracture — Master Project Checklist.md>) · [Course working draft](<./Docs/Floor Fracture Final Design Working Draft.pdf>)

## What the player sees

The player sees a connected board of smoky glass on the scanned floor. Passthrough shows the real floor between furniture and around the playable panes. A small ring marks the glass supporting the player’s estimated stance. Starting a level shows 3, 2, 1 and GO! in front of the player. The numbers disappear as play begins. Each breaking pane flashes amber once, shows its cracks briefly, then looks like clear glass. On the first level, all marked panes break at zero. Later, openings can appear around the starting pane for a jump, or rows can fall behind the player during a crossing. From a neighboring safe square, the player sees shards fall through a broken pane into the same 80 m chamber below every opening. Its distant surface reveals the destination of this level’s fall. Real walls and furniture have no app-drawn scan lines. When their support gives way, the fall animation lifts the surviving glass and the blue brick around the opening. Looking up reveals the original opening. The distant landing approaches, the view fades to black, and Results explains the loss and offers Retry Level, Choose level and Main menu.

## Why this Unity stack

| Approach | What it provides | Project trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Unity OpenXR, Unity OpenXR: Meta and AR Foundation | OpenXR input and Unity's AR abstractions for passthrough and captured room geometry. Portable gameplay code can run on another supported device with a room provider. | The room setup, floor-fit behavior, interaction assets and sample visuals would need separate integration. Device-specific capabilities still need device-specific verification. |
| Meta XR Core, Interaction and MRUK with Unity's Android OpenXR loader | The previous course exercises use these packages. MRUK supplies room anchors and floor polygons. The Floor Zone sample demonstrates a portal in a real floor. | Scene setup, permission flow and sample assets are Quest-specific. Board rules use independent coordinates and seeds so another room provider could reuse them. |

The project runs on Unity `6000.6.10f1`, upgraded on November 25 from the approved `6000.6.9f1`. The project uses URP `17.6.0`, Meta XR `209.0.0`, Unity OpenXR `1.19.0`, Unity OpenXR: Meta `2.7.0`, ARM64 and Android package `com.wa003.floorfracture`. Use the SDK's Building Blocks for the tracked rig, passthrough, controllers, MRUK and stone. One world-space menu receives Meta ray and fingertip poke events. The gameplay scene hides Room Guardian’s decorative scan mesh. MRUK still reads the floor and obstacles, and Meta’s environment-depth material lets foreground objects cover the practice stone. Project code selects valid footprints, decides wave outcomes and presents the glass and ending effects. ## First minute

1. Load the captured room and ask for spatial data when needed. While Meta locates the floor or finishes its outline, show the room through passthrough and keep the glass hidden. The game rechecks after the headset is put on, tracking returns or MRUK publishes a usable room. Welcome opens when the current localized room supplies both a floor boundary and a readable global scene mesh, and the game fits a connected board. **Check again** reloads the saved scan. **Space Setup** captures or updates the room.
2. Meta’s Find Spawn Positions suggests locations in the saved room. Aim for 69–75 cm panes and up to 12 playable tiles. If fewer than four fit, try smaller panes down to 40 cm. Two connected panes suffice. In **Play area → Board size**, choose **Entire connected floor** to use a larger connected area; the choice is saved and refits the current scan. The board can follow a narrow strip or wrap around furniture. Each pane and its 8 cm foot clearance must stay inside the mapped floor and clear furniture, walls, reliably raised geometry and measured drops. Meta’s Environment Raycast Manager checks visible panes before play. The fitted layout stays fixed during the run. Welcome reports the fitted shape and size. **Play area → Refit saved floor** uses the game’s hand controls. **Space Setup** opens the Quest scanner to edit walls or capture a room.
3. The menu appears 56 cm ahead and 24 cm below eye level. Point with either hand and pinch the highlighted row, touch it with a fingertip, or point and pull a controller trigger. A pinch held through a menu change must be released before choosing on the new page. Holding the Meta button brings an open menu back in front of the player. Tutorial starts with an untimed step. Find the ring underfoot, watch the starting glass crack, and step onto clear glass. Completing this move leads to the first wave when the player is ready. Optional stone practice lets a player pick up a rock, drop or toss it onto any intact pane to see it shatter, repeat a miss, or return to the game.
4. Stand on intact glass and press Start wave. The headset shows 3, 2, 1, then GO! The first Classic wave gives the player 2 seconds to reach a safe pane. Moving early keeps the original timer running.

## Wave rules

- Every wave schedules the starting square to break. An ordinary wave leaves a neighboring step, a row wave leaves an escape beyond the collapsing rows, and a jump wave leaves clear glass one opening away in every direction the fitted board supports. An isolated final jump landing completes the level.
- The ring and the verdict use the same support estimate. The headset tracks the eyes. The game uses the eye-to-neck offset to limit drift when the player looks down and keeps a planted stance across a narrow seam. At an existing opening, a circular 9 cm allowance preserves contact along an adjacent edge. The center of a hole remains unsupported. A frame arriving late interpolates the stance at the actual deadline.
- Possible outcomes at zero are supported by surviving glass, supported by breaking glass, and in a gap. Only the first survives. During the countdown a 0.3-second grace period allows crossing a gap, including a newly opened one. Leaving the board or losing tracking hides the glass and chamber during the countdown and during the Safe and Level clear displays. Show board brings back the glass once the room is recognized. Starting the next timer requires standing on intact glass. The score and completed level carry over. [Two boundary exits in the Meta simulator](<./Docs/Verification/Between-wave boundary/simulator.json>)
- Listen for the stress crackle coming from each pane as it flashes amber. Its tint, bright rim and cracks disappear together. Level one gives 0.80 seconds per clue, level seven gives 0.30 seconds and level twelve gives 0.25 seconds. On early levels, groups can start flashing up to 0.12 seconds apart.
- Manual starts give a three-second count-in. Ordinary movement windows open at 2.00 seconds on level one, 1.50 on level three and 1.00 from level six. Every required step gives at least one second from its own danger cue. The target share of surviving glass falls from 70% to 35% as levels advance, subject to keeping viable landings and exits.
- From level six, all marked glass can break at once, or a row can fall behind the player. The first row gives 1.25 seconds, the next gives 1.10, and from level nine a third can follow after 1.00. Each new row flashes and crackles as the preceding row drops. Shorter floors get shorter crossings.
- Jumps unlock from wave two of level two and wave three of level three. When a jump needs to open fresh gaps, the surrounding glass warns for a full second before falling. The player then has at least 1.85 seconds to cross one opening to any reachable clear landing. From level nine a second jump wave can follow. A pane that starts sliding moves into existing holes and locks at its exact position when caught. Up to one pane can slide per wave at levels three to five, two at levels six to eight and three from level nine, when they have separate paths with reachable exits. A wave can offer new sliders while the previous wave's slider is still on the board.


## Level hazards

Break timing is unchanged. The hazards below add movement and visual or audio clutter on top of it.

- Patrol panes, from level five, are moving panes whose lane turns a corner along a path of 3 to 5 cells. They stop where the player lands. From level seven roughly 1 in 9 moving panes is a patrol.
- Ride finale, from level six: some levels end with a ride instead of Last Tile. Every stationary pane breaks, the pane beside the player starts to slide, and the player steps onto it before it slides out of reach. Landing on it clears the level. The level six Ready screen hint reads "When no glass is left in reach, step onto the pane that starts to slide."
- Fog, from level two, is thick drifting fog below knee height. It thickens with level and partly hides the amber cracks. From level three, drifting fog patches also settle over single panes.
- Sweeping spotlights, from level four, are one to three overhead beams from lamps placed below the real ceiling. Their bright pools wash out cracks, and each lamp hums from its own direction.
- Lightning, from level five, is a double flash overhead followed by thunder from that direction, at most three flashes per second. With Reduced motion on it is one soft glow.
- Mix: fog, patches, spotlights and lightning are rolled per wave. Level one has none, levels two and three have zero or one, levels four to six have one (sometimes two), levels seven to nine have one or two, and from level ten there are two or three.

## Loss and replay

Full motion holds the shattered opening for 0.40 seconds, then the glass and shaft accelerate past the tracked eyes at 9.8 m/s². Glass fragments and wall ledges pass the viewer during the 80 m drop. Each level draws one of six destinations from a saved shuffle. Every open hole shows that destination until the level ends. The fall approaches that same surface. Shaft wind builds from a hiss to a roar with descent speed.

Water throws a crown of spray around the player, who plunges under 0.22 seconds after impact. The water starts white with churned foam and a column of entry bubbles rushes up past the eye, then it clears to blue-green with light overhead and bubbles rising. Lava flares and fills the view with ember red at 1.8 seconds. Two sticky pulls draw the viewer into Tar Pit over 2.5 seconds. The last glints close into black as the bubbling becomes muffled. At 2.3 seconds the sand fills the view with the color of the surface and darkens with depth. Space carries the falling speed through the exit for eight seconds, with the last second in darkness. Snow settles at waist height within 0.7 seconds. Sideways flakes lead into a whiteout at 2.8 seconds, then darkness. Water splashes, lava sears, tar glops, sand scatters and snow compresses with a cushioned thump. Entering space cuts the wind to silence while the descent continues. Three single knocks mark the protected opening count. A soft marimba note signals GO.

Reduced motion presents the landing from a stationary viewpoint. Pinching or pressing A or X skips the sequence. The left controller Menu button or Meta hand menu gesture opens Pause. Resume continues the countdown or fall. Main menu ends the run on the same fitted floor. Retry Level starts the lost level at wave 1 on fresh glass. Choose level opens every level reached in normal play. Unlocks save on the Ready screen and survive relaunch. [Quest playtest](<./Docs/Quest playtest.md>)

## Voice and results

Settings offers a default-off audition with three prerecorded, captioned lines for stepping, the optional stone and Results. They play locally. Movement, mute, Skip, disabling narration and Retry stop the voice. The Results caption now reads “Retry when you’re ready”; the recorded clip still says “Restart” until the line is re-recorded. Settings → Immersive room switches to full VR: passthrough turns off, a second Meta Effect Mesh draws the scanned global mesh (or labeled furniture volumes), the surrounding floor turns to stone, and hands switch from Real Hands to Meta’s stock hand material. Meta Spatial Audio places glass and impact sounds at their real game positions. Wais will choose the final voice after playing the revised game. [Offline narration and provenance](<./Docs/Offline narration.md>)

Results uses the cause recorded by the wave rule, including an expired gap crossing, an open hole at zero, a Memory warning or a breaking moving pane. In an isolated trial, Meta’s local LLM Building Block and SmolLM2 correctly identified one of three known losses. The deterministic result passed all twelve controls. The model ran on the Mac CPU and Metal with local game events. It added 157 MiB of model and tokenizer data to the trial. The Quest package uses the rule-based feedback and ships the three small voice clips. [Measured results and model responses](<./Docs/Verification/Offline coach experiment.md>)

## Passthrough around the playable glass

During play, the player sees the real room around the glass and in recesses where the board runs around objects. A broken pane still reveals the brick-lined shaft below it. The fall animation uses its original staggered stone, lighting and landing effects. Meta’s floor Effect Mesh supplies the animated floor, and Meta environment depth occludes game surfaces behind real objects. ## Where the game lives

Open `Assets/FloorFracture/Scenes/FloorFracture.unity`. Its **Floor Fracture** object has `GameFlow` and references the room provider and tracked rig. **Tools → Floor Fracture → Prepare game scene** reapplies the configured SDK blocks and keeps the main scene in the Android build list.

| Responsibility | Unity source and verification point |
|---|---|
| Room, permission and footprint | `RoomBoardPlacement` owns loading, permission recovery and relocation. Meta’s Find Spawn Positions suggests locations. `MrukFloorReader` supplies the floor and obstacle anchors. Adjoining level sections can support one board. A finer search checks angled aisles if the first layout fails. Each pane and its 8 cm foot margin must clear mapped furniture and confirmed obstacles inside the floor boundary. Gaps between floor triangles and small uncertain bumps are eligible for placement. The development recovery page reports the supported width or a live-depth rejection, and **Show / hide placement** reveals the evidence. [Building Blocks](<./Docs/Meta Building Blocks.md>) · [Placement tests](<./Docs/Verification/Observed floor placement.md>) |
| Tracked rig and direct grab | SDK Camera Rig, Passthrough, Controller Tracking, Interactions Rig and MR Utility Kit blocks are serialized in the scene. `Resources/PracticeStone.prefab` carries the SDK Grab Interaction block and a Rigidbody. `Scripts/Game/PracticeStone.cs` handles the optional practice, gravity, floor crossing and stone return. |
| Controller menus | The scene has one world-space canvas with Meta’s Ray Canvas and Poke Canvas Quick Actions. Both SDK surfaces dispatch to the same choices. `Scripts/Menu/GamePanel.cs` owns the menu choices, hover feedback and positioning. Desktop fixture input is Editor-only. |
| Rules and reproducibility | `Scripts/Rules/BoardState.cs` owns intact glass, score, wave pattern and deadline. `WavePlanner.cs` constructs connected, reachable survivors from a seed. `PlayerTileTracker.cs` projects the tracked neck estimate into board coordinates, keeps the marker and verdict on the same support, and reconstructs the deadline between frames. Each wave logs its seed, starting cell, safety choices and deadline. |
| Timing, decisions and replay | `Scripts/Game/GameFlow.cs` owns the countdown, scoring, level rebuild, pause, loss and restart. Interrupting a wave clears its unearned moving pane and warning. Show board waits for the headset to recognize the room. Starting again gives a full countdown and preserves previously earned glass and score. |
| Glass and shafts | `Resources/GlassTile.prefab`, `Scripts/Glass/GlassGeometry.cs` and `GlassTile.cs` share fitted warning and shard geometry. `GlassBoardView.cs` combines the stencil and exposed brick lips of adjoining openings. `ShardPool.cs` batches falling fragments into one mesh. Shader resources mask geometry beneath the real floor. |
| Endings and audio | `Scripts/Presentation/Endings/FallSequence.cs` selects a shuffled destination for each level. At loss it takes the player into that same chamber, then sequences blackout and Results. `FallingArena.cs` carries copies of the scanned brick meshes and the surviving glass past the stationary tracked rig. `FallScenery.cs` and `FallMeshes.cs` build the shaft and hazards, with original materials, recorded fall audio and the credited water sample. `GameSound.cs` plays the opening count and uses the stock Spatial Audio block prefab for positional impacts and echoes. |
| Measurements | `Scripts/Diagnostics/QuestRunMetrics.cs` records supported Unity counters in Android development builds. Pair its local CSV with the headset's OVR Metrics capture. [Performance guide](<./Docs/Quest performance.md>) |

Paths in this table are relative to `Assets/FloorFracture/`. The standing estimate uses the tracked eye and neck offset, with 3.5 cm of tolerance at an internal seam and up to 9 cm of circular support beside an existing hole. A jump allows at least 1.85 seconds from the opening to a landing, including up to 0.45 seconds crossing any valid gap from takeoff. Other holes have 0.30 seconds of grace. The deadline checks the actual supporting pane. A sliding pane spans 96.8% of the grid pitch, the same as stationary glass. It travels along a reserved strip of open floor and locks where the player catches it. [Rule and room tests](<./Docs/Verification/Floor entry rule tests.json>)
