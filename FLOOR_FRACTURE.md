## What the player sees

The player sees their real walls and furniture. Blue-gray shaft bricks fill the visible scanned floor around a rectangle of smoky glass. The furniture sits on the masonry, with floor visible between individual chair legs. A small ring marks the glass supporting the player’s estimated stance. A large countdown sits on a dark plate just beyond the board. On a long strip, it sits along the side within sight of the player. Cracked edges pulse while the timer counts down. At zero every marked pane breaks together. From a neighboring safe square, the player sees shards fall through a broken pane into the same 80 m chamber below every opening. Its distant surface reveals the destination of this run’s next loss. Real walls and furniture have no app-drawn scan lines. When their support gives way, the surviving glass and blue exterior bricks rise from the same place they occupied before the break. Looking up reveals the original opening. The distant landing approaches, the view fades to black, and Results explains the loss and offers Restart or Quit.

## First minute

1. Load the captured room and ask for spatial data when needed. The player can start Space Setup if the headset has no usable room.
2. Meta’s Find Spawn Positions proposes floor positions in the saved room. Fit the largest supported rectangle of full square panes on the scanned floor, up to the floor edge or a real obstruction. Measure the entire footprint, including its interior, and reject concave gaps, furniture and dividers. Try panes from 50 to 67 cm square with at least two rows and two columns, up to 64 panes in total. Prefer more square metres of playable glass, then the wider short side, then a larger pane. Welcome shows the columns, rows, pane size and measured footprint. Play area offers another spot and Space Setup.
3. Touch a menu button with a fingertip, point and pinch with a hand, or point and pull a controller trigger. Holding the Meta button brings an open menu back in front of the player. Tutorial starts with an untimed step. Find the ring underfoot, watch the starting glass crack, and step onto clear glass. Completing this move leads to the first wave when the player is ready. Optional stone practice lets a player pick up a rock, drop or toss it onto any intact pane to see it shatter, repeat a miss, or return to the game.
4. Stand on an intact pane and start the first Classic wave. Its five-second countdown gives the player time to find a stationary one-step route. Moving early keeps the original timer running.

## Wave rules

- Every wave schedules the starting square to break. At least one orthogonally adjacent stationary square survives. Surviving stationary panes stay connected, allowing the next wave to start from any survivor.
- The ring and the verdict use the same support estimate. The headset tracks the eyes. The game uses the eye-to-neck offset to limit drift when the player looks down and keeps a planted stance across a narrow seam. At an existing opening, a circular 9 cm allowance preserves contact along an adjacent edge. The center of a hole remains unsupported. A frame arriving late interpolates the stance at the actual deadline.
- Possible outcomes at zero are supported by surviving glass, supported by breaking glass, and in a gap. Only the first survives. During the countdown a 0.3-second trial grace allows crossing a pre-existing gap. Leaving the board or losing tracking pauses the wave and restores a view of the room.
- Classic shows the warnings until zero. Memory briefly reveals the same cracks and edge pulses, then hides them while the timer, ring and holes stay visible. Last Tile leaves a single reachable stationary square and awards the level. The board rebuilds and waits for readiness.
- The timer starts at 5 seconds and shortens by 0.4 seconds per level to a 2.5-second floor for the trial. Later levels add a low fog bank and an optional full-size pane that moves over two adjacent openings. It uses the ordinary glass face, rim and cracks, locks where the player catches it, and breaks into the same shards at its actual position on a later warning. Each wave retains a stationary escape. Practical reach and the final timing floor need wearer review.

## Loss and replay

On a full-motion loss, the pane shatters and the opening stays visible for 0.40 seconds. The tracked eyes, rig and room anchors stay in place. The surviving grid, scanned brick surface and shaft rise together from rest at 9.8 m/s². The opening stays registered to where the pane broke, including a loss near a corner. The 80 m descent takes about 4.04 seconds. Brick courses and projecting ledges should keep passing the player’s eyes for at least three seconds after the rim passes eye height. The hazard approaches from below. After contact, water slowly rises past eye height with bubbles and muffled sound, or flames and embers surround the player. Each full ending has a five-second landing sequence with a two-second fade to black before Results. Reduced uses a stationary reveal. Pinching or pressing A or X skips the sequence. The first opening reveals the next destination in the six-ending shuffle. It stays fixed across waves and previews. A loss consumes it. Water ripples, glowing lava channels, crystalline facets, stars, sand ripples or snowdrifts identify the landing before it approaches. Water keeps the same opaque ripple pattern through the descent and adds the Meta splash layer on contact. The Space sky fills both eye views behind the dissolving masonry. ## Voice and results

Settings offers a default-off audition with three prerecorded, captioned lines for stepping, the optional stone and Results. They play locally. Movement, mute, Skip, disabling narration and Restart stop the voice. Meta Spatial Audio places glass and impact sounds at their real game positions. Wais will choose the final voice after playing the revised game. [Offline narration and provenance](<./Docs/Offline narration.md>)

Results uses the cause recorded by the wave rule, including an expired gap crossing, an open hole at zero, a Memory warning or a breaking moving pane. In an isolated trial, Meta’s local LLM Building Block and SmolLM2 correctly identified one of three known losses. The deterministic result passed all twelve controls. The model ran on the Mac CPU and Metal with local game events. It added 157 MiB of model and tokenizer data to the trial. The Quest package uses the rule-based feedback and ships the three small voice clips. [Measured results and model responses](<./Docs/Verification/Offline coach experiment.md>)

## Brick around the playable glass

The same dry blue-gray stone runs across the real floor outside the glass, down neighboring broken panes and along the deep fall shaft. All three surfaces use the same staggered 54 × 27 cm bricks, mortar and face color. Shading gets darker down a hole and a nearby hazard can light the walls. On the scanned floor, the joints continue across patches. Only the glass supports a player during a wave.

Meta’s MRUK **Effect Mesh** builds the surface from floor anchors. Meta’s **Occlusion** block supplies live per-eye depth so a couch, chair legs and a trash can appear in front of the brick. The floor shader compares the current depth capture with one recent capture reprojected to the same physical floor point. A changing depth, a pause or an unavailable capture discards the older sample. Open space between chair legs and the spot a bin just vacated reveal the floor finish. Quest shows the real floor while the depth provider is unavailable. The glass footprint, including holes, stays open through the surround. Wall treatment awaits Wais’s review. [Meta floor meshes](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/unity-mr-utility-kit-manage-scene-data/) · [Meta depth mask sample](https://developers.meta.com/vr/documentation/unity/unity-sample-depth-api/)

The first brick build’s Editor checks cover a couch profile, the gap between two chair legs and two locations for a bin. In the standalone simulator, the High-ceiling Room supplied 25 anchors and fitted a 5 × 8 board. Wais saw his furniture in front of the bricks and confirmed menu recentering. He requested more playable glass, a deeper fall and blue brick matching the shaft. The revised High-ceiling Room fits 6 × 9 panes at 58.7 cm. ## Where the game lives

Open `Assets/FloorFracture/Scenes/FloorFracture.unity`. Its **Floor Fracture** object has `GameFlow` and references the room provider and tracked rig. **Tools → Floor Fracture → Prepare game scene** reapplies the configured SDK blocks and keeps the main scene in the Android build list.

| Responsibility | Unity source and verification point |
|---|---|
| Room, permission and footprint | `Scripts/Room/RoomBoardPlacement.cs` on the MRUK instance owns loading, permission recovery, automatic placement and relocation. Meta's Find Spawn Positions offers floor candidates. `MrukFloorReader` reads floor and obstacle shapes. `BoardFitter` validates the full playable rectangle. The Rec Room fixture is Editor-only. [Complete Building Blocks review](<./Docs/Meta Building Blocks.md>) |
| Tracked rig and direct grab | SDK Camera Rig, Passthrough, Controller Tracking, Interactions Rig and MR Utility Kit blocks are serialized in the scene. `Resources/PracticeStone.prefab` carries the SDK Grab Interaction block and a Rigidbody. `Scripts/Game/PracticeStone.cs` handles the optional practice, gravity, floor crossing and stone return. |
| Controller menus | The scene has one world-space canvas with Meta’s Ray Canvas and Poke Canvas Quick Actions. Both SDK surfaces dispatch to the same choices. `Scripts/Menu/GamePanel.cs` owns the menu choices, hover feedback and positioning. Desktop fixture input is Editor-only. |
| Rules and reproducibility | `Scripts/Rules/BoardState.cs` owns intact glass, score, wave pattern and deadline. `WavePlanner.cs` constructs connected, reachable survivors from a seed. `PlayerTileTracker.cs` projects the tracked neck estimate into board coordinates, keeps the marker and verdict on the same support, and reconstructs the deadline between frames. Each wave logs its seed, starting cell, safety choices and deadline. |
| Timing, decisions and replay | `Scripts/Game/GameFlow.cs` owns countdown, exactly-once scoring, level rebuild, pause, loss and restart. It tells the presentation what happened. Focus or tracking loss during a wave cancels the countdown. |
| Glass and shafts | `Resources/GlassTile.prefab`, `Scripts/Glass/GlassGeometry.cs` and `GlassTile.cs` share fitted warning and shard geometry. `GlassBoardView.cs` combines the stencil and exposed brick lips of adjoining openings. `ShardPool.cs` batches falling fragments into one mesh. Shader resources mask geometry beneath the real floor. |
| Endings and audio | `Scripts/Presentation/Endings/FallSequence.cs` prepares the next shuffled destination when glass breaks. At loss it takes the player into that same chamber, then sequences blackout and Results. `FallingArena.cs` carries copies of the scanned brick meshes and the surviving glass past the stationary tracked rig. `FallScenery.cs` and `FallMeshes.cs` build the shaft and hazards, with original material and audio resources plus the credited water sample. `GameSound.cs` schedules centered ticks, directional impact and echo using the stock Spatial Audio block prefab. |
| Measurements | `Scripts/Diagnostics/QuestRunMetrics.cs` records supported Unity counters in Android development builds. Pair its local CSV with the headset's OVR Metrics capture. [Performance guide](<./Docs/Quest performance.md>) |

Paths in this table are relative to `Assets/FloorFracture/`. The standing estimate uses the tracked eye and neck offset, with 3.5 cm of tolerance at an internal seam and up to 9 cm of circular support beside an existing hole. The timer allows 0.30 s to cross a gap and checks the actual support at zero. For the trial, levels start at 5 seconds and shorten by 0.4 seconds to a 2.5-second minimum. A sliding pane spans 96.8% of the grid pitch, the same as stationary glass. It travels between the centers of adjacent holes and locks where the player catches it.
