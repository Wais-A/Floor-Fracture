**What the finished game should look like.** Put on the Quest and you see your real walls and couch. Staggered, matte bricks with recessed mortar run across the visible floor to the room edges. Chair legs, the sofa and a trash can sit in front of that surface. A rectangular grid of smoky glass occupies a clear patch in the brickwork. You step onto one, and a small marker shows which square the game thinks you occupy. A countdown begins. Several edges start pulsing and branching cracks spread across the glass. You move to a sound tile. Nothing drops early. At zero, the damaged panes break into pieces that tilt, catch the light, and fall past a blue-brick lip. On intact glass you can look through any broken square into the same deep chamber. Rippling water, glowing lava or one of the other four destinations is already visible there. If you lose, that surface approaches as the walls pass you. The next countdown plays on the glass that remains. Eventually there is one last safe tile. Reach it and the board rebuilds for a harder level. Lose and the shaft walls rise around you. At the bottom might be water that slowly passes over your eyes, lava that surrounds you with flames, or another surprise. The picture fades to black, then you see your result, Restart, and Quit.

**Agreed rules that future work must preserve.** The starting tile breaks every wave, so standing still cannot win. Ordinary stepping must always work. The tile under the headset determines occupancy. All scheduled glass breaks happen at zero. Holes persist throughout the level. Classic and Memory mix during the shrinking sequence. Last Tile finishes each level. Sliding tiles lock after landing. A brief grace period allows crossing an existing hole. The six endings are independent of the gameplay rules. Multiplayer is a future possibility.
